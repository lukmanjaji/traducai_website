How it works

Three steps, and the room speaks every language.

  1. 1

    Connect the room's sound

    Plug the TraducAI box into the sound desk, or open the microphone page on a laptop in Chrome. Share your slides too, so names and numbers come out right.

  2. 2

    People scan the QR code

    Put the room's QR code on the screen, the badge or the table. Listeners open it in their phone's browser and choose their language. A PIN keeps private rooms private.

Features

Built for the people on stage, in the seats and at the sound desk.

The speaker's own voice

Each translation is spoken in a voice cloned from the speaker as they talk, not a robotic narrator. When the room sound is too poor to clone well, it switches to a natural standard voice by itself.

Captions for everyone

The original and the translation, sentence by sentence, with a colour for each speaker. Good for noisy rooms, people who are hard of hearing, and the 31 languages offered as captions.

Projector captions

A full-screen caption view for the big screen: up to four languages side by side, large text, and a QR code so the room can switch to phones.

Your human interpreters too

Interpreters work from a browser booth: they hear the floor, see the AI captions, and press Go on air. Their voice replaces the AI voice for that language until they hand back.

Slides as context

Share the presentation from the microphone page. TraducAI reads each new slide so names, acronyms and figures are interpreted correctly.

Glossary per room

Add up to 1,000 terms: organisation names, programme titles, technical words. You can edit it live while the session runs.

Transcripts and minutes

Download each day's transcript per language. One click writes minutes in the language you choose: summary, key points, decisions, action items and open questions.

Opening times

Set when each room is open. Outside those times listeners see when it opens next and everything switches off, so nothing runs overnight.

Pay for what is heard

A language only runs while someone is listening to it, and stops a minute after the last listener leaves. Silence is not charged.

For organisers

One dashboard for every room.

See who is listening, in which language, how far behind the speaker it runs and what it costs, live. Create rooms, print their QR codes, set PINs and opening times, and invite interpreters, all from the browser.

For venues with interpretation systems

Works with the headsets you already have.

Many venues own an infrared or radio interpretation system, with a receiver and headset for every seat. TraducAI plugs into it the way an interpreter booth does: each language comes out of its own sound output at the sound desk and goes into its own channel of the transmitter. People turn to their channel, and anyone can still use their phone.

  • Infrared, radio and tour-guide systems
  • One cable per language, two languages per stereo output
  • Test beeps to find each channel on a receiver
  • Runs in Chrome or Edge, nothing to install

    Questions

    Good to know.

    Do listeners need to install an app?

    No. They scan the QR code, the page opens in their phone's browser, and they choose a language. Earphones are all they need. Keeping the screen on keeps the sound going.

    Can we use the interpretation headsets we already have?

    Yes. TraducAI can send each language to its own channel of an infrared, radio or tour-guide interpretation system. A computer at the sound desk plays each language on its own sound output, cabled into the transmitter where an interpreter booth would go. Receivers and phones work side by side.

    What is a language-hour?

    One hour of speech interpreted into one language. A 1-hour meeting interpreted into French and Arabic uses about 2 language-hours, less once pauses are left out. Listening in the speaker's own language, and the number of listeners, do not count.

    What happens when my hours run out?

    You get an email at 80% and at 100% of your hours. A session that is running can finish (up to one more hour); new sessions wait until you add hours, or extra hours are charged at your plan's rate if you turn that on.

    How far behind the speaker is it?

    About three seconds, measured in real sessions. Most of that is the time the model needs to hear enough of a sentence to translate it well.

    What do we need in the room?

    An internet connection and a feed of the room's sound: either the TraducAI box connected to the sound desk, or a laptop with Chrome on the microphone page.

    Can our own interpreters work with it?

    Yes. Send an interpreter a booth link. They hear the floor, see the AI captions, and go on air for their language whenever they want; their voice replaces the AI voice until they hand back.

    How accurate is it?

    Very good for clear speech, and better with a room glossary and shared slides. It is still machine interpretation: for negotiations, legal or medical sessions, put a human interpreter on air for the languages that matter most.

    Who can get into a room?

    Anyone with the link, unless you set a PIN; the QR code you print already includes it. Rooms are created only by approved accounts, and each has its own microphone key.

    What happens to the transcripts?

    Captions are saved as daily text transcripts per language, which the room owner can download or turn into minutes. Minutes are written by AI, so read them before you share them.
